what to do in what order, and why, given a ~5-8 hr/week budget and an 18-19 month runway

from the original 18 months to absorb a planned baby buffer — see Phase breakdown).

## Guiding principles

1. **Family stability comes first.** The baby due January 2027 and the eventual Prescott move
   are the actual reasons this plan exists — when a study goal and family bandwidth conflict,
   bandwidth wins and the plan slips, not the other way around.
2. **Patience over calendar.** No debt and a fully-funded 6-month emergency fund already exist,
   so there's no financial pressure forcing a rushed landing. The ~18-19 month mark is a
   readiness target, not a deadline — if the only remote offers available when you get there fail
   the Employer vetting checklist (recent PE/distressed ownership, recent layoffs, confirmed
   offshoring pattern — see the Blackboard/Anthology case), extend the Carvana stay rather than
   take the least-bad option. One more short stint stacked on Coupa would hurt the *next* search
   far more than a few extra months at a stable job costs now.
3. **Bank the closest win first.** AWS Pro is 80% done — finish it before starting anything
   new, or that knowledge decays and the motivation hit is wasted.
4. **Let the day job pay for the Azure ramp.** You're in Azure/.NET 40+ hrs/week already;
   study time should go to formalizing/certifying that, not re-learning it from scratch.
5. **One flagship portfolio project beats five tutorials.** Certs get you past ATS/recruiter
   screens; a real multi-cloud, event-driven reference architecture is what carries a senior-level
   interview loop and gives you something to write about.
6. **Visibility is a thread, not a phase.** Start it once there's something real to show
   (~month 6), and keep it lightweight and recurring rather than a late scramble.
7. **AI-driven development is a force multiplier, not a topic.** Use Claude Code to build the
   portfolio project faster, and let that experience itself become a talking point — 2026
   senior/platform loops increasingly probe for agentic/AI-assisted engineering fluency.

## Positioning & market timing

Decided after talking through the realistic odds of this plan landing a remote role:

- **A note on how to read the market, so future-you doesn't drift into panic or complacency:**
  "tech is dying" is not an accurate read — what actually happened is a correction after the
  2020-2022 over-hiring bubble plus a real RTO wave, which shrank the *ratio* of remote-to-total
  postings and made remote roles more competitive per opening, not extinct. The Prescott-specific
  version of this concern is real and different: once relocated, remote is genuinely close to the
  only option for this level of comp, since there's no meaningful local senior-tech market there.
  That's a legitimate geographic constraint driving this whole plan — it doesn't require believing
  the broader industry-death narrative to justify taking it seriously.

- **Target Senior IC as the primary lane — not as a fallback from Staff/Principal, but because
  it's actually the best fit for what's wanted.** The real goal is remote + stable + well-paid +
  good WLB + engaging, not a title. Three reasons stack in favor of Senior as the lane to get
  there: (1) applying Senior-to-Senior is a lateral move from the current Carvana title, so it
  doesn't reopen the "why did your title go down" question the way a Staff/Principal application
  does; (2) total YoE (~7 now, ~8.5-9 by the end of this plan) is a hard filter at a lot of
  companies for Staff+ titles regardless of actual scope/skill — no amount of certs or portfolio
  work changes a YoE cutoff; (3) Staff/Principal roles carry more scope-ownership pressure and
  organizational politics, which cuts against the WLB and stability goals directly. Don't
  self-select out of Staff+ postings that explicitly welcome IC-track senior architects, but
  stop treating Staff/Principal as the target and Senior as the fallback.
- **Coupa stays on the resume**, compressed to one neutral, specific line ("the role turned out
  to be governance/SecOps rather than hands-on engineering, which isn't what I wanted, so I moved
  back to platform/backend engineering"). This is genuinely an easy one — it's specific,
  self-aware, and hard to argue with, not something to be defensive about in an interview. Omitting
  it is a background-check discovery risk (rescinded offers post-hire) that's worse than the
  short-stint optics itself.
- **The market is currently slow/frozen** — treat that as validation for the build-first,
  approach rather than a reason to rush or panic. Warm intros are the highest-leverage channel
  whenever hiring does loosen up, and relationship-building doesn't require an open req to start
  paying off later.
- **Realistically, nothing job-search-related starts before the baby buffer ends.** Original
  thinking was to start low-pressure networking immediately; the actual answer is no applying
  before January, and likely not for a few months after — paternity leave and adjusting to being
  a new father come first, full stop. Casual/opportunistic activity (recruiter-only "open to
  work" on LinkedIn, taking an occasional inbound conversation as free interview practice) can
  restart once things feel sustainable post-buffer, tentatively **Phase 3** — but a deliberate
  push still waits for the certs/portfolio to exist (~month 9+), and there's no fixed date
  attached to any of this beyond "when it's actually sustainable."

## Alternative path: startup full-time (optionality, not a decision yet)

The fractional startup work has a second possible future beyond being a resume differentiator:
if it keeps growing, it could become a real full-time option instead of (or as well as) landing
a remote role elsewhere. No need to bet on this now — the current light/fractional scope already
lets it mature in parallel with the main plan without forcing a choice.

**Concrete conditions before full-time there is even on the table** (not vague "if it feels
stable," actual gates):
- ~18 months of runway covered by revenue/burn rate — ideally profitability, not just runway
- Offers a Roth 401k
- Offers health insurance

Until the startup clears those, treat it strictly as fractional/light-touch, exactly as now —
family risk (insurance, the baby, no cushion to spare) rules out going full-time before then
regardless of how promising the traction looks. Revisit this around the same ~18-19 month mark
as Phase 6 of the main plan, when there'll be real data on both paths instead of a guess made too
early on either one.

## Employer vetting checklist (do this before accepting, not after)

Built specifically because the last two job changes didn't screen for this and it cost real
time and stress. Offshoring risk and culture-mismatch risk both had visible signals ahead of
time — Instructure's PE ownership was public before the offshoring/RTO squeeze; Coupa's culture
was almost certainly visible on Glassdoor/Blind before accepting. Run this on every serious offer
in Phase 6, not just the ones that feel uncertain.

### Ownership & financial health (strongest predictor of future offshoring/RTO)
- [ ] Public, founder-led, PE-owned, or recently-taken-private? Search "[Company] private equity" / "[Company] acquired" — recent PE ownership or a recent take-private is the single biggest red flag, independent of sector or remote policy
- [ ] Any layoffs in the last 12-18 months? Check layoffs.fyi and recent news
- [ ] Recent leadership turnover — multiple VPs/CTO departing within ~12 months is a leading indicator, not a lagging one (this is exactly what preceded the Instructure situation)
- [ ] If public: skim the last couple earnings calls/investor materials for language like "cost optimization," "global delivery," "right-shoring," "efficiency initiatives"
- [ ] LinkedIn headcount trend for the company and specifically for the engineering org — growing, flat, or shrinking? Are recent engineering hires concentrated in lower-cost geos relative to where this role would sit?

### Team & role specifics (ask directly in interviews)
- [ ] "How is the engineering org distributed geographically, and has that shifted in the last 2 years?"
- [ ] "What's this team's hiring plan for the next year?"
- [ ] "Why is this specific req open — backfill, growth, or restructuring?"
- [ ] How decomposable is the role itself — commodity ticket work offshores easily; deep, system-specific platform/architecture work doesn't. Push for a role description that's clearly the latter.
- [ ] How long has the hiring manager been in their current role, and how long has the team existed in its current form?

### Culture & WLB reality check
- [ ] Glassdoor/Blind check specifically for recent (last 6-12 months) reviews — old reviews are stale, recent ones reflect current reality
- [ ] Ask for (or arrange informally via network) a conversation with a peer-level engineer outside the formal interview loop — hiring managers and recruiters are not neutral sources on WLB
- [ ] Ask directly about on-call load, meeting density, and typical working hours — vague or deflected answers are themselves a signal

### Quick-scan red flags
- Recently taken private / recent PE acquisition
- Multiple senior leadership departures within the past year
- Layoffs in the past 12 months
- Job postings emphasizing a "global delivery center" or aggressive offshore hiring
- Inconsistent or vague answers across multiple interviewers about team structure or why the role is open

### Quick-scan green flags
- Stable ownership (public without activist pressure, founder-led, or durably profitable) with no recent take-private
- Headcount flat-to-growing, not shrinking
- Long-tenured leadership and a team that isn't in constant reorg
- Consistent, specific answers across interviewers about team structure and hiring plans

## Coding & SQL drills (ongoing, ramps up in Phase 5-6)

A different, perishable muscle from system design/certs/portfolio — don't bank it 18 months
early, maintain it lightly throughout, then ramp hard right before actually interviewing.

- **Phases 1-4 (maintenance mode):** 1-2 problems every couple of weeks, rotating across
  **Java, C#, and TypeScript** — kept at "clean, explainable, right complexity" difficulty
  rather than grinding hard problems.
- **Phases 5-6 (ramp-up):** shift to 3-4x/week dedicated practice, since this is the actual
  deployment window.
- **SQL gets equal or higher priority than generic algorithm practice** — you've already hit it
  in real loops, and it's increasingly weighted as heavily as DS&A at Senior+ backend/platform
  level.
  - **LeetCode's SQL section** for interview-pattern recognition (joins, aggregations, window
    functions, common question shapes) — fine for timed pattern practice, but schemas are
    synthetic and it won't show query plans or indexing behavior.
  - **For genuinely realistic practice:** load a real sample database — Sakila (MySQL), Chinook
    (MySQL/Postgres/SQLite), or a Postgres port of Northwind — into the local Postgres/MySQL
    container already set up per `TOOLING-LOCAL-FREE.md`, and write increasingly complex queries
    against it, including `EXPLAIN ANALYZE` for indexing/query-plan practice. This tests real
    join complexity and query optimization, which LeetCode's synthetic schemas can't.
  - **PGExercises.com** as a free middle ground — one realistic schema, progressively harder,
    real PostgreSQL.
