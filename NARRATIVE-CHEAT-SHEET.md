# Interview Narrative Cheat Sheet

Four transitions, four short answers. Rehearse these out loud until they're boring to say —
boring means confident, not evasive. Keep each to ~15-20 seconds unless asked to go deeper.
Never volunteer more than what's asked. Never speak negatively about a former employer, even
when the honest reason involves one.

## 1. Parchment → Instructure
**Say:** "Parchment was acquired by Instructure, so that was really one continuous run — five
years total, going from tech lead to software architect."

- Frame as one employment relationship through an acquisition, not two jobs.
- If pushed for detail: the acquisition is a matter of public record, so there's nothing to
  over-explain here — state it plainly and move on.

## 2. Instructure → Coupa
**Say:** "After three years as an architect, Instructure went through a return-to-office mandate
around the same time the company was contracting post-acquisition — a lot of the leadership I'd
worked with, including several VPs and the CTO, moved on in that same window. It was a natural
point to look elsewhere."

- Factual, external, verifiable — not a complaint. Don't editorialize about the company beyond this.
- If pushed further: "the org was in a lot of flux at that point" is as far as this needs to go.

## 3. Coupa → Carvana
**Say:** "The Coupa role turned out to be governance and SecOps-focused rather than hands-on
engineering, which isn't what I was looking for, so once I recognized that, I moved back into a
pure engineering role at Carvana."

- Specific and self-aware. Owns the mismatch without dwelling on it or sounding defensive.
- Do **not** say "I hated it" or anything emotionally charged — the factual version is stronger
  and already fully defensible on its own.

## 4. Carvana → next role (the one you're interviewing for)
**Say:** "Carvana was a deliberate trade — better total comp at a point where my family needed
stability and savings velocity, with a baby on the way. I've been remote for most of my career
and always intended to return to it once that was in place — this move is that."

- Leads with the *reason* (family, comp, timing), not with "I don't like being in-office," which
  can read as a complaint rather than a plan.
- If asked "why leave now": tie it back to the same reason — the temporary trade has served its
  purpose, and returning to remote was always the plan, not a reaction to something going wrong.

## Bonus: if asked about current architecture experience
**Say:** "Alongside my day-to-day role, I've been a fractional software architect — fully remote
— for a pre-seed startup for about eight months, cleared with Carvana during hiring, which has
kept me hands-on with architecture-level decisions and current, active remote work even while my
day job is in-office."

- Use this whenever a title-trajectory question comes up ("why Senior now after Architect?") —
  it's live, current, external proof that the substance never actually went away.
- No need to defend the startup's odds of survival — that's not the point being made here.
- **Updated line, now that there's real traction (Sept 2026):** "...and the startup has since
  landed real contracts — including a guaranteed deal and a likely one in the $25-40k ARR range
  — with more in the pipeline, including MassMutual evaluating a paid tooling buildout." Concrete
  and citable, not a hedge — this is real revenue, not just early-stage promise.

## Master framing (if asked to summarize the whole arc)
"Five years building up to architect, a four-month detour into a role that wasn't the right fit,
and a deliberate, temporary in-office trade for comp and family stability. I'm now looking for
where all of that lands long-term: stable, remote, and genuinely engaging."

## Rules for delivery
- Shortest true answer wins. Resist the urge to over-explain or pre-empt questions that weren't asked.
- If a follow-up genuinely digs deeper, answer the specific question asked — don't re-deliver the whole arc again.
- Never bad-mouth Instructure, Coupa, or Carvana by name in tone, even while being factually honest about circumstances.
- Practice these out loud, not just silently in your head — the goal is for them to sound unrehearsed.
